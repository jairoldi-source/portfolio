The AI analyst doesn't know what it doesn't know

Unbounded AI reporting looks exactly as confident whether the comparison group is valid or not. That's the whole problem.

I'm trying out this format as a way of recording what I'm thinking about in analytics. Feedback welcome - here's the first one.

Causal inference looks rigorous. The math is the easy part. The hard part is comparison group selection - which requires world knowledge, domain expertise, and enough imagination to see what's missing from the data, not just what's in it.

AI is very good at the math. It's structurally weak at the missing-data problem, because it doesn't know what it doesn't know - and it will answer just as confidently regardless. A competitor launch during your test window. A news story that broke mid-study. A player segment that was already churning before you touched anything. None of that shows up in the dataset, and nothing about a clean regression output tells you it's missing.

The distinction I keep coming back to is unbounded versus bounded AI reporting. Unbounded: point a model at the data, get conclusions back. It's confidently incomplete - it looks exactly as polished whether the comparison group is valid or not, and there's no visible seam between a sound analysis and a broken one. Bounded: a human domain expert has structured the question, chosen the comparison, and defined what the model is and isn't allowed to conclude. That's a genuinely different, useful thing.

The analyst who's been burned by a bad comparison group develops scar tissue - the caveat they reflexively add, the thing they instinctively double-check the next time. A model doesn't carry that forward. It starts every analysis exactly as confident as the last one, whether the last one was right or not.

This is the whole argument behind the Analytics Intake Tool. The point isn't to automate analysts out of the loop. It's to be honest about which parts of the job are actually safe to hand to a probabilistic system, and which parts need a person who's been wrong before and remembers why.

Confidently incomplete is worse than uncertain.
