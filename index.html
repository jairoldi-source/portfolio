Approach

Right math + wrong question = wrong result.

Ask for average spend on the site, and you've already decided that's the right lens on the real question. We've moved from "I don't need data to decide" to "I must have data to decide" - but that's not the same as being data-informed. Data-informed means the analyst is in the room from the start, making sure the question is right, not just the math.

Why the industry doesn't matter

Games and fintech don't share customers, regulators, or a line of code. What they share is this: someone asks for a number, and the number they asked for isn't quite the number they need. That gap is the job, wherever it shows up — which is why the same instincts kept working when everything around them changed.

What changes
  • The vocabulary — DAU and whale segments vs. AUM and compliance windows
  • Which questions are even legal to test
  • How fast the organization is used to moving
What transfers
  • Turning a vague request into a decision someone can actually make
  • Knowing the subgroup that got averaged away is usually where the real answer lives
  • Building trust in the data before the organization has any reason to doubt it

I've spent fifteen years or so trying to figure out what questions are worth asking of data - not just pulling the answers, but knowing which problem is actually worth solving. That skill has carried across two industries with almost nothing else in common: games and fintech. What transferred wasn't domain knowledge, it was asking questions in a goal-focused way, working back from what actually matters.

Data is most powerful when it's asking the right question — not when it's confirming the answer someone already had.

Journey

Career journey.

It wasn't a straight line. Seven stops, a winding path, and the lesson each one left behind.

Water Management District → M.S. Math
The Beginning: Following the Data

I initially planned on working in environmental policy, with my first job at a Water Management District in Florida. Entry-level jobs there happened to be managing water quality data. Being out on the water scooping up samples was fun, but being at my desk building a Microsoft Access database was even more fun. I decided to go back for a more quantitatively oriented master's degree, and stepped from there into the world of tech.

Data Infrastructure Systems Thinking
Lesson carried forward
What you end up loving to do might not be what you had imagined it would be.
IT Consulting · 1997–2011
The Long Runway: IT Consulting

In my long stint in IT Consulting, I became the person who made sure the application we were building was really answering the business needs. This meant becoming great at learning new domains quickly, and at the time also meant jumping in on all the data work. It was the unglamorous, carrying-water part of the project, but it fit my goal-oriented personality well to keep things moving forward. Over time, I realized I loved solving problems with data more than coding widgets.

Stakeholder Alignment Requirements Translation
Lesson carried forward
Understanding how people will use your product is essential to making something lasting.
Zynga · 2011–2013
Finding My People: Games and Analytics

I met someone at a party who ran a game company and thought it sounded like the most fun group of nerds I could work with. I was right, and I was so lucky to be able to learn data analytics with a groundbreaking group of fantastic people at Zynga, my first games job. I launched a half-dozen games, absorbed an amazing amount of analytics, and learned that disciplined focus is what protects a team's energy and ensures analytics efforts directly move the core mission forward.

Prioritization Player Analytics

Big Huge Games · 2013–2019
Building From Scratch: Games Strategy

At Big Huge Games, I was the first data person and the first product manager, both producing the analysis and consuming it. We built DomiNations, a $175M mobile strategy game still running today. It was a lot of learning on my feet to do both roles at once, and I'm immensely grateful to my first direct reports for helping to really forge the way.

0→1 Build Team Development

Facet Wealth · 2019–2022
The Mission Problem: Fintech

I joined Facet to work somewhere with a mission, democratizing financial planning for people outside the traditional income brackets. I was the first data hire, taking us from Excel and chewing gum to a production staff and fantastic team. The highest-value work wasn't just technical, it was building an organizational culture where data guides strategy rather than rubber-stamping pre-existing assumptions.

Trust & Change Management Fintech
Lesson carried forward
Building trust in data is as important as building the data itself — small cracks in confidence become craters.
Electronic Arts / Tiburon · 2022–2026
The Biggest Build: Transforming a Studio

I walked into a team that had been leaderless for six months, two of five analysts gone before my first major launch, and we delivered more with a skeleton crew than the full team had the year before. I transitioned the team from reactive order takers to strategic thought partners by establishing a structured intake framework focused on decision intent, and over my four years shifted the ethos from reactive to proactive — so that we were able to do deep dives that drove actual change and didn't just confirm hypotheses.

Team Leadership Process Design
Lesson carried forward
